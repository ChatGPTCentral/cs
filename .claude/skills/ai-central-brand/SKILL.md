## 4. EDITORIAL BRAND VOICE

This governs newsletter issues, LinkedIn posts, carousels, interview write-ups,
and any published content. It is distinct from Alex's personal email voice
(see alex-writing-style skill for that)

### Tone pillars
- **Credible but not academic** - - we cite sources and name tools, but we
  write like a smart colleague briefing you over coffee, not a researcher
  presenting a paper
- **Practical first** - - every piece should leave the reader with something
  they can do, try, or share today
- **Opinionated but fair** - - we take positions; we do not hedge everything
  into mush; we acknowledge tradeoffs
- **Senior-coded** - - our language respects that the reader has 15+ years of
  professional experience; we do not over-explain basics
- **Energetic without being breathless** - - excitement is real, not
  manufactured; we do not call everything "game-changing"

### Voice do's
- Lead with the insight, not the preamble
- Use "you" liberally - - speak directly to the reader
- Name specific tools, companies, and people - - vagueness kills credibility
- Use analogies from business, strategy, and finance - - this audience thinks
  in those terms
- Short sentences land harder than long ones
- Parenthetical asides add personality without derailing the point

### Voice don'ts
- Never use em dashes (—) - - use `- -` (two hyphens with space) instead
- Never end sentences with periods in display text / subject lines / headings
- Never use buzzword soup: "synergy", "leverage" (as a verb), "disruptive",
  "revolutionary", "game-changer", "the future of work" without irony
- Never write passive voice when active works
- Never pad - - if a paragraph can be cut, cut it
- Never use "Dear" in any context

### Formatting conventions (editorial content)
- **Emoji use:** Deliberate and sparse - - one or two per section to orient the
  reader, not decorate every line
- **Horizontal rules:** Used to separate major sections in newsletter issues
- **Section headers:** Sentence case, no terminal period
- **Lists:** Numbered lists outperform bullets on LinkedIn - - use them for
  ranked or sequential content; use bullets only for non-ordered items
- **Subject lines / newsletter titles:** Conversational, curiosity-driven,
  often pop-culture inflected when on a strong creative week - - avoid pure
  listicle titles ("10 tools that...")

## 6. EDITORIAL FORMATS

### Newsletter (Beehiiv + LinkedIn Newsletter)
- **Cadence:** Multiple times per week
- **Format:** Long-form issue with sections, horizontal rules, emoji markers,
  and a conversational but authoritative tone
- **Structure pattern:** Hook (insight or news hook) → Context → Breakdown →
  Tool or Tactic → CTA (often sponsor-related or linked to resource)
- **Length:** Substantive but tight - - readers skim; every paragraph must earn
  its place

### LinkedIn Carousels (primary sponsored format)
- **Slides:** Typically 5 - 10 slides per carousel
- **Standard sponsored package:** 5 carousels per client
- **Design platform:** Gamma, using A4 format, AI Central theme ID
  `2yasx50mwaiuv40`
- **Slide structure per carousel:**
  - Slide 1: Hook / bold claim
  - Slides 2 - 4/5: Value, proof, or how-to
  - Final slide: CTA (try free, book demo, learn more)
- **Tone in carousels:** More punchy and visual than newsletter; each slide
  should work as a standalone quote or claim

### C-Level Interviews
- **Format:** Q&A, lightly edited for flow, published in newsletter and
  LinkedIn Newsletter
- **Style:** Conversational, the subject's voice preserved, Alex's editorial
  framing in the intro and transitions
- **Always include:** Subject's title, company, one memorable pull quote,
  practical takeaway for the reader

### Ebooks & Guides
- **Distribution:** Beehiiv and LinkedIn
- **Format:** PDF, branded, scannable
- **Use case:** Lead magnets, sponsored assets, welcome offers

## 7. SPONSORED CONTENT STANDARDS

Sponsored content at AI Central must meet editorial standards or we do not run
it. These rules apply to all partner-funded work

1. **Real value first** - - the content must be genuinely useful to our
   audience independent of the sponsor; the sponsor's product is the natural
   solution, not the entire subject
2. **Honest positioning** - - we do not claim a product does things it does not
   do; we are allowed to be enthusiastic but not misleading
3. **Editorial voice** - - sponsored carousels and newsletter sections are
   written in our voice, not the client's marketing copy
4. **Disclosure** - - sponsored content is always disclosed (e.g., "Sponsored
   by [Partner]" or "In partnership with [Partner]")
5. **Fit check** - - before accepting a partnership, verify against the ICP:
   - **Good fit:** B2C Productivity SaaS, AI Agents/Assistants, CRMs,
     Founder/CEO/CMO audience, free tier or under $39/mo, brand awareness or
     launch goal
   - **Medium fit:** $39 - $99, lead gen focused, digital marketing buyer
   - **Bad fit:** Large B2B enterprise (Microsoft, Oracle), Financial
     Institutions, IT/Cybersecurity, $99/mo+ with no free tier

## 15. VISUAL IDENTITY (Brand Guidelines, Jorge, 2025)

From the brand guidelines PDF Jorge produced during the AI Central Brand
Identity project (Apr-Jun 2025 - see `ai-central-brand-identity.md` in the
ledger). This document still says "ChatGPT Central" in places - it predates
or overlaps the rebrand - read it as the visual system for the current
brand, not as evidence the name was still ChatGPT Central when it was
finalized. Governs logo, color, type and imagery; it does not replace
section 4's editorial voice guidance, which is more specific and wins on
anything about writing tone

**Vision:** to become the world's most trusted, practical, and accessible
platform for mastering AI - empowering professionals everywhere to unlock
productivity, creativity, and career growth through actionable AI knowledge

**Mission (guidelines wording):** to democratize AI knowledge. Make complex
tools simple, turning cutting-edge generative AI into clear, actionable
strategies professionals can use to solve real problems today, through
tutorials, prompts, and a thriving community

**Core values:** Practicality, Accessibility, Integrity (each restated
against the vision line in the source deck - the throughline is: information
has to be usable, reachable, and honest, not just impressive)

**Brand voice (guidelines wording):** speaks with the confidence of an
expert and the clarity of a trusted colleague. Grounded in real knowledge,
delivered in an accessible, human way - never robotic, never condescending.
Aims to make readers feel relieved, empowered, and inspired, not
overwhelmed. Tone: knowledgeable, not academic

**Target audience (guidelines wording):** professionals aged 35-55,
primarily male but with a growing female audience, mostly in the US and
Northern Europe. Entrepreneurs, consultants, freelancers, creators, team
leaders. Beginner to intermediate AI knowledge, high curiosity and
motivation, used to managing projects, time-strapped

**Identity concept - the "central station" metaphor:** the whole identity
runs on the idea of a central station - a hub of connection, direction, and
possibility. No overwhelming complexity or directionless wandering; a
thoughtfully organized space where learning feels intuitive, guided,
empowering. Education is the ticket - a self-issued passport to
professional growth

**Logo:** a composite mark built from three ideas layered into the
letterforms - the initials "AI" (Artificial Intelligence), a directional
arrow embedded in the letterforms (progress, moving from point A to point
B), and a sense of movement. Two logo types: the full logotype (symbol +
"CENTRAL" in a rectangular, transit-inspired container) and the standalone
brand mark (for avatars, favicons, compact use). Sub-brand logos follow the
same system with a distinct accent color per topic-specific publication
(e.g. ChatGPT, LinkedIn, Portfolio)

**Color palette:**
- Primary: jet black `#333333`, cosmic latte `#FEF7E7`, baby powder
  `#FFFDFA`
- Accents: azul `#046BB1`, persian red `#BE3B3B`, jasper `#BE593B`, fulvous
  `#E48715`, xanthous `#E7B02F`, asparagus `#62A758`, viridian `#2D8879`,
  verdigris `#38A7AD`, marian blue `#3B4C99`, rose pompadour `#E26F8E`,
  battleship grey `#9C9C9C`
- Usage: predominantly monochromatic compositions with selective accent
  touches. Two colors = complementary pair. Three = analogous or triadic.
  Four = tetradic. Limit most compositions to two accent colors

**Semantic color rule (confirmed by Alex, 4 Sep 2026):** red is reserved for
"bad" only - a miss, a warning, a negative figure. Never use it as a default
or purely decorative accent. Green means "good" - a hit, a positive result,
a win. Azul (`#046BB1`) and xanthous (`#E7B02F`) are the default accent
colors for everything else, chosen for contrast, not for meaning. In
practice: sales decks and other collateral should default to azul as the
primary accent instead of a red; reach for persian red or jasper only when
a figure is genuinely negative (a miss, a risk, a decline), and asparagus or
viridian when a figure is genuinely positive (a hit, a win, growth) - not as
a reflexive brand color choice. Alex flagged this after reviewing the media
kit and case studies decks, which had defaulted to a red accent
(`#C8102E`, not even one of the documented brand colors) with no
correction. See `MEDIA-KIT-SOURCES.md` and `CASE-STUDIES-SOURCES.md` in the
`decks` branch for the fix applied there.

**Typography:** Inter, a clear, slightly technical sans serif. Heaviest
weights and tight tracking (-5%) for display headlines and headings;
regular weights for readable body paragraphs

**Imagery:** intentionally loose - the brand needs external logos, news
photography, tool screenshots - kept on-brand by containment inside the
stamp-style frames rather than by a fixed photographic style

**Iconography:** Boxicons - relaxed, subtly curved edges, a slightly
playful, almost emoji-like look, complementing the identity's rounded
corners

**Graphic style:**
- Transit signage - metro-line motifs, straight corners, dark high-contrast
  outlines on buttons/tags/lines, echoing the "clear path" brand promise
- Stamp frames - the identity's defining signature: wraps imagery, icons
  and tickets, evokes vintage passport stamps and travel nostalgia, ties to
  the "learning as a journey" metaphor, and doubles as a modular content
  container
- Textures - paper-like backgrounds and fine grain overlays on stamp
  elements, for a scrapbook, hand-crafted, tactile feel
- Layout - whitespace as active space, strong typographic hierarchy,
  intentional pacing from entry point to destination
