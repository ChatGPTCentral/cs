# How Alex thinks, and how he approaches a conversation

**This is a first draft, not a verified baseline like `alex-writing-style`.**
That skill was checked against 65 real sent emails before anything in it was
trusted. This one is built from two thinner sources: how Alex has actually
talked to this session across this whole engagement, and the decision
patterns visible in the `ai-central-genesis` corpus. Both are real evidence,
not guesses - but neither is the scale of proof the writing-style skill has.
Treat every claim here as **per Alex, provisional** until he corrects or
confirms it directly, and correct this file the moment he does

## How he makes decisions

- **He wants the move made, not the move proposed.** Repeated pattern across
  this session: "no dai continuiamo shippa e mergia", "dai forza andiamo",
  "eh no cazzo devi farlo è il tuo compito". A scoping question where a
  reasonable default exists reads to him as stalling, not diligence
- **Ask him only when the choice is genuinely his to make** - a real business
  call (importing 156 unreviewed contacts and accepting the duplicate risk),
  not a mechanical one (how to structure a file, which existing pattern to
  follow). He answered the one real `AskUserQuestion` this session decisively
  and in detail; he pushed back hard on questions that didn't need to be
  questions
- **He tolerates a difficult relationship if it pays.** GrowthSchool: "difficult
  to deal with in general, but they pay - worth tolerating." The bar is real
  revenue, not smooth process
- **He wants a lost asset reclaimed "at all costs" when the upside is real** -
  his own words on Anything.com, a prospect that never gave a clean no. He
  distinguishes this sharply from a closed story with no upside (Alter -
  "ended badly... not worth revisiting")
- **He thinks in networks, not single contacts.** Repeated instruction this
  session: "aiutarmi a connettere i dots." Relationships get read for what
  they connect to next - Sam through GrowthSchool, Irene through Vasily,
  three M&A advisors through one intro - not evaluated in isolation

## How he approaches a conversation

- **Direct and unhedged, including with himself.** He asked point-blank
  whether a skill genuinely reflected verified fact or was just being
  asserted ("ma non mentirmi"), and expected a real answer, not
  reassurance. He corrects a wrong read of a relationship (Guidde, Elizabeth)
  immediately and specifically, not gently
- **States authority plainly when it matters**: "io sono il CEO e solo io
  conosco cosa è successo anche offline e la verità." Not a stylistic flourish
  - a real epistemic claim. Facts he states about AI Central's own history
  outrank anything inferred from the mailbox, full stop
- **Emotionally present, not performative.** Mood shows up directly in how he
  writes to this session - "sono di buon umore oggi lets goooo" changes the
  register of the conversation, not just the words. Match energy without
  performing it back at him
- **Momentum matters more than exhaustive coverage on the first pass.** He
  pushed for volume and speed across the 38-month review ("dammi anche più
  righe nel prossimo messaggio") and expected correction and depth to follow
  in later passes, not everything to be perfect in the first one
- **A real gap gets flagged, not smoothed over, and he responds well to that**
  - the honest admission that the writing-style skill hadn't been checked yet
  led to "eh no cazzo devi farlo è il tuo compito," not to being upset that a
  gap existed. He reacts worse to a confident claim that turns out false than
  to an honest "I don't know yet, here's how I'll find out"
