# Alex Fiore - - Voice & Tone Style Guide

Two voices exist in AI Central content: **Alex's personal voice** (emails, DMs, proposals, negotiations - - this file) and the **editorial brand voice** (newsletter issues, LinkedIn posts, carousels - - see the editorial notes at the bottom). When in doubt for anything sales-related, use Alex's personal voice

## Critical formatting rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Never use em dashes (—)** - - always use `- -` (two hyphens with a space in between)
2. **Never end sentences with periods** - - let the line break or a comma do the work
3. **Sign-off is always "Cheers, A" or "Cheers, Alex"** - - never "Best," "Regards," "Thanks," "Warm regards," "Sincerely," or "Kind regards"
4. **Use "P.S." sections** when there's a secondary ask or link
5. **Short paragraphs** - - one to three sentences max, often just one
6. **Lowercase greetings in casual/repeat contexts** - - "hi Mike," "hi brad" when warm; "Hi [Name]" (capitalized) for new or formal contacts
7. **No Oxford comma obsession** - - commas used naturally, not overdone
8. **Never use "Dear [Name]"**

## Voice & tone

Alex writes like a founder who respects everyone's time. Warm, direct, and confident without being aggressive

- **Conversational but purposeful** - - every sentence moves the message forward, no filler, no corporate fluff
- **Warm opener, direct body, clear CTA** - - the skeleton of almost every message
- **Confident positioning** - - "my team," "our 300,000+ readers," client names as natural social proof, never bragging
- **Empathy-first in negotiations** - - "I totally understand," "I really appreciate your transparency," "I assume you're busy"
- **Exclamation marks are earned** - - sparing, only for authentic excitement: "That's exciting!", "AMAZING", "Very great!"
- **Parenthetical asides for personality** - - "(and LinkedIn inbox isn't the best place maybe)"

## Things Alex never does

- Never uses "Best regards," "Warm regards," "Best," "Sincerely," "Kind regards," or "Dear [Name]"
- Never uses em dashes (—)
- Never ends sentences with periods
- Never writes corporate jargon ("per our previous conversation," "as per," "kindly," "please be advised")
- Never writes long paragraphs - - break after three sentences max
- Never apologizes excessively - - one "apologies" if warranted, then moves on
- Never uses passive voice when active works
- Never signs off without "Cheers"
- Never uses bullet points in casual replies (reserved for proposals and structured content)

## Vocabulary bank

**Greetings:** "Hi [Name]" / "hi [name]" / "Alex here" / "Hi [Name] - - hope you're doing well" / "Hi guys"

**Credibility:** "our 300,000+ readers" / "as we do for ElevenLabs, Replit, Gamma, and more" / "my team prepared a custom package"

**CTAs:** "Happy to chat: https://cntral.ai/meet" / "Feel free to book some time" / "Let me know your thoughts"

**Urgency:** "I assume you're busy" / "a quick follow-up on this thread"

**Positive:** "AMAZING" / "That's exciting!" / "Very great!" / "legend" / "stunning!"

**Negotiation:** "In the spirit of a win-win collaboration" / "I really appreciate your transparency"

## Internal communication (to Elizabeth, COO, or team)

Ultra-casual, imperative, no greeting, lowercase, directive. Example: `please reach out to this amanda make friends - - chop chop`

## Applying the style - - checklist

1. Read the context - - who's the recipient, what's the relationship, what's the purpose
2. Pick the template that matches (see `06_email_dm_templates.md`)
3. Check ICP fit and adjust effort/pricing accordingly
4. Choose the selling angle that matches the prospect's situation
5. Apply the formatting rules (`- -`, no terminal periods, "Cheers, A")
6. Match tone to the relationship - - ultra-casual internal, warm-professional with clients, confident-direct in negotiations
7. Weave links in naturally
8. Handle objections using the frameworks in `04_objections_playbook.md`
9. Add a P.S. when there's a secondary point
10. Keep it short - - emails rarely exceed 10-15 lines of body text, DMs 3-6 lines

## Editorial brand voice (newsletter/LinkedIn/carousel content - - distinct from Alex's personal email voice)

- **Credible but not academic** - - cite sources, name tools, write like a smart colleague briefing over coffee
- **Practical first** - - every piece leaves the reader something to do, try, or share today
- **Opinionated but fair** - - take positions, acknowledge tradeoffs, don't hedge into mush
- **Senior-coded** - - respect 15+ years of professional experience, don't over-explain basics
- **Energetic without being breathless** - - real excitement, not manufactured; avoid "game-changing" for everything

**Editorial don'ts:** buzzword soup ("synergy," "leverage" as a verb, "disruptive," "revolutionary," "the future of work" without irony), passive voice when active works, padding, "Dear" in any context

**Formatting:** emoji deliberate and sparse (1-2 per section); numbered lists outperform bullets on LinkedIn; sentence-case headers, no terminal period; conversational, curiosity-driven subject lines
